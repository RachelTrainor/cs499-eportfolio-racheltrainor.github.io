Welcome to My ePortfolio

Welcome to my CS499 Computer Science ePortfolio. This portfolio highlights the skills and experience I gained at Southern New Hampshire University, along with the improvements I made to my weight-tracking Android application for my capstone project. Through my coursework, I have explored several areas of computer science, including software development, databases, algorithms, and information security. The projects and reflections included here demonstrate my technical growth, the challenges I have worked through, and how I have applied what I have learned throughout the program.

Professional Self-Assessment

When I first started my Computer Science degree at Southern New Hampshire University, I knew I wanted to pursue a career in technology, but I did not have a clear idea of exactly where I wanted to end up. Computer Science covers so many different areas, and I was interested in more than just programming. Throughout the program, I have had the opportunity to explore software development, cybersecurity, networking, databases, and other areas of technology. Rather than narrowing my interests to one specific field, my experiences have made me more interested in learning how these different areas work together.

Earning my degree while working full-time has been one of the more demanding but gratifying things I have done. There were times when balancing work, school, and personal responsibilities was difficult, but it taught me how to manage my time, stay organized, and work through challenges. I have also learned that I can figure things out even when I do not immediately know the answer. I believe this is one of the most valuable things I will take with me into my career, especially in a field where technology is constantly changing.

Software Engineering and Databases

Software development has been one of the most hands-on parts of my education. Through different projects, I have learned how applications are designed, developed, tested, and improved. I have also learned that writing code is only one part of the process. The way an application is organized, how it handles information, and how easily it can be maintained are all important.

One project involved developing a full-stack travel application that combined a user interface, server-side functionality, and a database. This helped me understand how applications communicate with databases and how information moves between different parts of a system.

Another project involved creating a three-dimensional scene using OpenGL. This introduced me to a different side of programming where I had to work with lighting, textures, and object placement to create a visual result. Although this project was different from building a traditional application, it helped me improve my problem-solving skills and understand how programming can be applied in different ways.

I have also worked with relational and NoSQL databases, which helped me understand the importance of organizing information and choosing appropriate methods for storing and retrieving data. These projects taught me that software development is not just about making something functional, but also making sure it is organized, reliable, and practical to maintain.

Algorithms and Data Structures

My understanding of algorithms and data structures has also developed throughout the program. Before learning more about these concepts, I did not fully understand how much the way information is organized could affect an application. Through coursework and projects, I have gained experience with different ways of storing, sorting, searching, and processing data. I have also learned that there are often multiple ways to solve the same problem, and that choosing an approach involves reviewing factors such as efficiency, readability, and maintainability.

Being able to apply these concepts to actual projects has helped me better understand their importance. Instead of only focusing on whether a solution works, I now try to consider whether it makes sense for the application as well as how it might affect future changes.

Security and Risk Awareness

My concentration in Information Security has influenced how I approach technology and software development. Throughout my coursework, I have learned about protecting information, identifying vulnerabilities, access control, and reducing security risks.

I have also worked on projects involving secure software development and explored practices such as encryption, hashing, and secure communication. These experiences helped me understand that security should be considered throughout the development process rather than something added after a project is completed.

My background in Asset Protection has also given me a different perspective on security. Although physical security and cybersecurity involve different systems, both require attention to potential risks, protection of resources, and ensuring access is limited to those who need it. I believe this combination of educational and professional experience is something that can help set me apart as I pursue opportunities in technology.

CS 499 Capstone and ePortfolio

For my CS 499 capstone, I chose to enhance Weigh to Go, an Android weight-tracking application I originally developed earlier in the program. I selected this project because it gave me an opportunity to revisit something I had already created and improve it using the knowledge and experience I have gained since then.

Looking back at my original work was interesting because I could recognize areas that I would approach differently now. While the application already has its main functionality, there were opportunities to improve its structure, add useful features, and strengthen how information was managed.

For my software design and engineering enhancement, I focused on improving the application's overall reliability and maintainability. This included strengthening input validation, code organization, and making changes to how certain features and permissions were handled.

My algorithms and data structures enhancement focused on giving users more ways to view and understand their information. I added sorting options and progress calculations that made the application more useful beyond simply recording weight entries.

For my database enhancement, I improved how the application stores and retrieves information, particularly by connecting weight records to individual user accounts. This helped create a better structure for managing user data and reinforced the importance of keeping information properly organized.

Although these enhancements focused on three different areas of computer science, they all contributed to improving the same application. The software engineering changes made it more reliable, the algorithms provided more useful functionality, and the database improvements strengthened how information was managed. Together, they demonstrate how different technical concepts can work together to create a more complete application.

Developing my ePortfolio has also helped me improve how I communicate and present my work. Through my code review, written narratives, and GitHub Pages website, I have practiced explaining technical decisions and showing how my projects have improved. I wanted my portfolio to demonstrate not only what I created but also what I learned throughout the process.
